Privacy Policy

Rabbit Ears · Effective November 17, 2026

Who we are

Rabbit Ears is published by Legitimate Enterprises. It is a media server that a household runs on its own Mac, and apps for the web, Mac, iPhone, Apple TV, Android and Roku that play from that server.

The short version

Legitimate Enterprises runs no Rabbit Ears server and receives no data from you. Your accounts, viewing history and media stay on the server your household runs. The apps have no analytics, no advertising, no tracking and no crash reporting of their own.

What the apps send, and where

The apps talk only to the Rabbit Ears server whose address you enter, or that they find on your local network. They send it your sign-in, what you browse and play, your playback position, and what your device can play: its formats, screen, audio output, network type and connection speed. The server uses this to choose how to stream to you. No app sends anything to Legitimate Enterprises or to any other company.

Some screens show links to other sites, such as The Movie Database or YouTube. Nothing is sent to them unless you open the link.

What the apps keep on your device

The server's address, a sign-in token, and display choices such as sort order. The token is kept in the Keychain on Apple devices, encrypted with the Android Keystore on Android, in the app's own storage on Roku, and in the browser's local storage on the web. Signing out removes the token; removing the app removes the rest.

What the server keeps

The server stores its data on the household's own machine. That data includes each person's name; each adult's email address and password, stored only as a hash; optional PINs, also hashed; each kid's optional birthday and viewing filters; the household's devices, with their name, type and when each was last seen; watch history, playlists, collections and stations; and logs. It also keeps the titles, artwork and descriptions it fetched for the household's media. The household's admin controls this data. Legitimate Enterprises cannot see it.

Who the server contacts

To describe the household's media, the server looks titles up with:

  • The Movie Database (TMDB), for movies and TV, using the household's own TMDB API key;
  • MusicBrainz and the Cover Art Archive, for music.

These requests carry the names of titles, artists and albums found in the household's files, and the server's internet address. They carry no account or viewing data. If the household's admin sets up single sign-on, the server also contacts the sign-in providers the admin chose. The server contacts no one else. Each of these services has its own privacy policy.

App stores and devices

Apple, Google and Roku run the stores and devices the apps are on, and collect data under their own privacy policies. If you choose to share crash data with app developers in your device's settings, Apple may pass crash reports for Rabbit Ears to Legitimate Enterprises.

Children

Kid profiles are created by an adult in the household. They have no email address or password, and their data stays on the household's server like everyone else's.

Deleting your data

Your data is on your household's server, so deletion happens there. The server's admin can delete any account from the admin web UI. In the web client, the household's owner can remove other adults, and adults can remove kids. Deleting an account deletes its watch history and playlists. Legitimate Enterprises holds no data about you, so there is nothing for us to delete.

Security

The apps use https to reach a server outside your local network. On your local network they may use plain HTTP, which is not encrypted.

Changes

If this policy changes, the new version will be posted on this page with a new effective date.
